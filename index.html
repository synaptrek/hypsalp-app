Support

Found a problem, or have an idea for HypsAlp? Write to hypsalp [at] tuta [dot] io.

The quickest way is from inside the app: Settings ▸ Send Feedback. This opens a pre-filled email in your Mail app containing only the app's name and version. You review the message and send it yourself; nothing is sent automatically.

Please don't include other people's personal information in your message.

Privacy policy

In short: HypsAlp does not collect any data. Everything you record or save stays on your iPhone, and nothing leaves it unless you export or share it yourself.

No accounts, no tracking, no servers
There is no account, no advertising, no analytics, no tracking and no third-party SDK: the app uses only Apple frameworks. It works entirely offline and makes no network calls at all. Its App Store privacy label is "Data Not Collected". The developer cannot see any of your data and receives nothing from you unless you choose to write an email.
Legal basis
Because the app collects and transmits no personal data, the developer does not process any and no legal basis under the GDPR is needed for the app itself. The only processing the developer carries out concerns the emails you may send (see below).
On-device only
The app keeps, in its private storage on your phone, only what you create while using it: recordings (pressure, fused altitude, the pressure trend, the temperature and humidity of an optional external sensor and, when location is on, GPS positions and the distance computed from them), organized in folders; saved places and custom markers with their precise coordinates (your tent, your car); measurements and alerts (slope and height measurements, snow profiles and the weather alerts that fired, with their time, values, notes and position where available); the last hour of altitude and the pressure-trend history; the altimeter calibration, the sea-level pressure it learned, the air temperature you entered and the calibrations of the experimental acoustic and wind tools; and your settings (units, which tools are shown, the chosen external sensor).
Backups and sync
Like any app, HypsAlp's data may be included in the iCloud or computer backups you make of your iPhone, under the terms of those services. The app itself does not sync anything through iCloud. You can also export a backup of the app's data as a single file and import it again; importing only adds, it never overwrites.
How long data is kept
Your data is kept until you delete it. Only the last hour of altitude and the pressure-trend history expire on their own as they age.
Deleting data
Settings ▸ Manage Data deletes by category and shows what each category holds before you confirm. Erase All Data removes everything, offering to write a backup file first. Deleting the app removes everything stored on the phone.
Permissions
Each permission is requested only by the feature that needs it, and the app keeps working without it. Motion & Fitness reads the barometer for altitude and pressure trends. Location ("When In Use") provides the GPS altitude that corrects the barometer and the position during recorded hikes; background location is used only while a hike is being recorded, because the phone is usually in a pocket. Camera lets you aim at a slope and, with LiDAR, measure its angle; it is also used by the experimental rangefinder and for the flashlight in the Morse SOS. Nothing is recorded or saved. Microphone is used only while a tool that needs it is open: the thunder after a lightning flash, the echoes of the acoustic thermometer, the rush of the wind. Sound is turned into measurements on the spot and never recorded or saved. Bluetooth is used only if you choose an external temperature sensor (a RuuviTag or a BTHome sensor); until then the app never touches Bluetooth and never asks. Background Bluetooth is used only during an active recording, only to read the chosen sensor, and the app only reads: nothing is ever written to the sensor. Notifications are local: weather alerts at night, the turnaround reminder, battery warnings, a paused-recording reminder, arrival at a target altitude.
Lock Screen, Live Activity and widget
During a recording, a Live Activity and a widget show your altitude and recording data on the Lock Screen. They are displayed by iOS on your device and transmitted nowhere.
When data leaves your phone
Data leaves your phone only when you explicitly export or share it, through the share sheet or the Files app: CSV, GPX, CAAML and A4 PDF exports of recordings, saved places and snow profiles; a shareable picture of a recording; a backup file; and the emergency card text, which contains your coordinates and is meant for rescuers. Two switches protect you: Include Location in Exports is off by default, and without it the CSV, GPX, CAAML and PDF exports carry no coordinates; Shared Pictures can hide the start and/or the end of a track within a radius of 200 m to 2 km, so a picture does not reveal your home or the trailhead. Diagnostics pages never carry your position, and the sensor diagnostics report cannot be shared at all.
When you email the developer
If you send an email, the developer receives your email address and the content of your message, and uses them only to reply to you and to fix the problem you report. This is done on the basis of the developer's legitimate interest in supporting the app. Messages are kept for as long as needed to handle your request, and no longer than 12 months, unless the law requires otherwise. They are not shared with anyone or used for any other purpose. You can ask for them to be deleted at any time by writing to hypsalp [at] tuta [dot] io.
Your rights
The only personal data the developer may hold is the emails you send. Depending on where you live, you may have the right to access, correct or delete that data, to object to its processing, and to lodge a complaint with your data-protection authority. In the EU/EEA these rights come from the GDPR; in Italy the authority is the Garante per la protezione dei dati personali. To exercise any right, write to hypsalp [at] tuta [dot] io.
Children
HypsAlp is not directed at children, and it collects no data from anyone.
Third-party data in the app
The app includes offline datasets from third parties, bundled as static files: the Copernicus DEM GLO-30 elevation model (Copernicus WorldDEM-30 license; relief images carry the attribution the license requires), surveyed points from OpenStreetMap (© OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL 1.0) and NOAA's World Magnetic Model WMM2025 (public domain). The bundled dataset of summits, saddles, passes, huts and bivouacs is a Derivative Database of OpenStreetMap data, released under the Open Database License 1.0 (ODbL) and available at github.com/synaptrek/hypsalp-app. The full attributions are in Settings ▸ Data & Licenses.

Terms of use

An instrument and a notebook, not a verdict
HypsAlp is an aid for the mountains. It is not a certified navigation, meteorological or safety device, and it does not replace a map, a compass, training, or judgment in the field. Altitude, pressure trends, slope and height readings, daylight and way-back times are indications to be checked against the conditions and your own judgment. The app gives no arrival-time estimates.
Storm warning
The storm warning is a hint from the barometer, not a forecast: a fast fall in pressure, or the pressure jump typical of a gust front. Afternoon heat thunderstorms can give no barometric signal at all.
Snow profile
The snow profile is a notebook. It records your own observations and does not assess snowpack stability or avalanche danger; it does not replace the avalanche bulletin, training and judgment on the terrain.
Lightning, oxygen, estimates
The lightning distance is an indication, not an alerting service: the first thunder comes from the nearest point of the channel. The oxygen reading is physical information, not medical advice. Freezing level and sun-and-shade hours are estimates: the real lapse rate varies, winter inversions throw it off, and the 30 m elevation model knows nothing of trees, buildings or boulders.
Emergencies
The emergency card helps you read your position and places calls through the normal phone dialer. It sits beside the iOS Emergency SOS and satellite SOS and does not replace them: in an emergency, use the official emergency features of your phone and call the rescue number of the country you are in.
Experimental tools
Tools marked as experimental are off by default and provided for curiosity and testing. Their results may be inaccurate and must not be used for safety-related decisions. Some tools, or parts of them, are hidden or reduced on iPhones without the hardware they need, such as LiDAR.
Coverage
The elevation model and the surveyed points cover the Alps, the Apennines, Corsica, Sardinia and Sicily (roughly 36–49° N, 5–19° E). Outside that area the app still works as a barometric altimeter and says so with a "Partial Coverage" or "Outside Coverage" badge; Settings ▸ Altimeter ▸ Data Coverage lists it country by country.

The app is provided “as is”, without warranty of any kind. Readings, calculations and derived views may contain errors, and the app may stop working or lose data. Keep your own backups of what matters to you.
